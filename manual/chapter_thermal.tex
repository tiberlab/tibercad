\chapter{Heat Balance simulation}
\section{Theory}
The theoretical model of the heat balance problem can be found in Refs. \cite{wachutka}. In the following a brief treatment is reported.

\subsection{Heat Equation}
The equation implemented is 

\begin{equation}\label{heat_law}
-\nabla \cdot\left(\kappa_{tot} \nabla T \right) = H_J + H_P
\end{equation}

where $T$ is the temperature, $\kappa_{tot}$ is the total thermal conductivity tensor, $H_J$ is the thermal source due to Joule's effecr and $H_P$ is the source due to Peltier's effect. 

\subsection{Thermal conductivity}

The term $\kappa_{tot}$ is  

\begin{equation}\label{k_tot}
\kappa_{tot}=\kappa_L + \kappa_n + \kappa_p
\end{equation}

where $\kappa_L$ is the lattice thermal conductivity whereas $\kappa_n$ and $\kappa_p$ are related to electrons and holes respectively. 

The lattice thermal conductivity model can be constant or temperature dependent. In the latter case the following model are implemented (Ref. \cite{kmodel})

\begin{equation}\label{k_T}
\kappa(T)=\frac{1}{a + bT + cT^2}
\end{equation}

The carriers thermal conductivity are computed by the Wiedmann-Franz law
\begin{equation}\label{k_n}
k_n = \sigma_n L T \nonumber
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{k_p}
k_p = \sigma_p L T \nonumber
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the Lorenz number ($2.45\cdot 10^{-8} W*\Omega /K^2$) and $\sigma_n$, $\sigma_p$ are the electronic conductivity of electrons and holes, respectively.

\subsection{Joule's effect}

The heat source due to Joule's effect is 

\begin{equation}\label{Hj}
H_J=-\nabla \cdot\left(\varphi_n \mathbf{J_n} + \varphi_p \mathbf{J_p}\right)
\end{equation}

where $\varphi_n$ is the electro-chemical potential of the electrons and $\mathbf{J_n}$ is the electron current density.\\
The terms $\varphi_p$ and $\mathbf{J_p}$ are the analogous quantities related to holes. 

\subsection{Peltier's effect}

The heat source due to Peltier's effect is 

\begin{equation}\label{Hj}
H_P=\nabla\cdot\left(T \mathbf{J_n}P_n + T \mathbf{J_n}P_n\right)
\end{equation}

where $P_n$ and $P_p$ are the thermoelectric powers of electrons and holes, respectively.

The thermoelectric power models implemented are the constant one and the \textit{diffusivity} model. 
The diffusivity model is

\begin{equation}\label{Pn}
P_n = -\frac{k_b}{q}\left[\frac{5}{2}+  \frac{T}{\mu_n} \frac{\partial\mu_n}{\partial T} +  \frac{q\varphi_n + E_c}{k_b T} \right]
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{Pp}
P_p = \frac{k_b}{q}\left[\frac{5}{2}+  \frac{T}{\mu_p} \frac{\partial\mu_p}{\partial T} -  \frac{q\varphi_p + E_v}{k_b T}  \right]
\end{equation}

where $k_b$ is the Boltzmann constant, $E_v$ is the edge of the valence band, $E_c$ is the edge of the conduction band, $q$ is the electron charge, and $\mu_n$ and $\mu_p$  are the mobilty of electrons and holes, respectively.

The diffusivity model neglets the phonon's drag and until now works only for non degenerate case.
